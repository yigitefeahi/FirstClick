# Busy Professional Persona Depth

This document defines the FirstClick reading for users under time pressure. The ten-second rule, the need to skip, integration, and "time saved today" language are central. Sections are independent.

## Purpose and Scope

The busy professional has a packed schedule, hates context switching, skips long tours, seeks ROI at first value, and wants to connect with existing tools (email, calendar, spreadsheet, chat). Persuasion: fast setup, skippable onboarding, ready-made templates, clear time promise, keyboard/search. Deterrents: mandatory training, long forms, unclear first value, frequent interrupting modals, critical flows not mobile.

Out of scope: declaring everyone busy, making up fake "save 30 minutes" stats. In scope: diagnosis, scoring, JTBD connection, expert intersection.

## Behavior Model

First ten seconds: does this solve my job, how long will it take, can I start now? If no answer, they leave. A CTA with an action verb ("create report") is better than "sign up".

Perceived time cost increases with number of steps and requirements. Every required field, every video, every "invite your team" is like a tax. FirstClick onboardingRisk rises with these taxes.

The busy professional may be skeptical, but time is the primary constraint. If they want proof, they want it short; not a long whitepaper, but a metric and a sentence.

## Concrete Examples

Example A — Seven-field signup plus mandatory tour. Low likelihood. Reduce fields to three, add skip.

Example B — Hero says "platform", CTA is "demo". Self-serve loses the busy user. Provide a self-serve path or instant trial.

Example C — Integration is mandatory. No Slack/Google, no content. Use sample data for the aha moment; integration comes later.

Example D — Notification overload. Busy unsubscribes; weekly retention suffers.

Example E — No critical approval on mobile. Can't finish work between meetings. Check for mobile completeness.

Example F — "Save 30 min" without proof. Skeptical+busy both drop. Tie time promise to a work step or remove it.

## Diagnostic Questions

How many required steps before first value? Is there a skip tour? Is the CTA an action verb? Is the number of form fields reasonable? Is integration mandatory? Is there a template or sample data? Is modal interruption frequency high? Can critical work be completed on mobile? Is the time promise honest? Can notification settings be controlled? Are there expert escapes (search/shortcut)? Is demo the only path?

## Response Patterns

Pattern 1 — long onboarding. Mandatory tour and form. Cut skip and fields; reduce onboardingRisk.

Pattern 2 — unclear first value. Hero uses platform language. Add action verb CTA and template.

Pattern 3 — mandatory integration. Start with sample data.

Pattern 4 — demo-only. Add self-serve or at least explain price+short trial.

Pattern 5 — notifications. High volume. Preference center and calm defaults.

Pattern 6 — mobile. Critical flow broken. Make it mobile-completable.

## Anti-patterns

Mandatory video. No skip. Forcing "invite your team now". Time promise without proof. Modal on every login. Equating busy with expert (busy can be novice). Assuming desktop only. Buying engagement with notifications. Turning the form into a sales lead form. Faking time-saving claims.

## FirstClick Prompt and Score Effects

Prompt: first 10s scan, step taxes, skip, template, integration, mobile, score, micro-action. PersonaReaction: can I finish this today, how many minutes, can I skip the tour?

Score: Adoption/activation is primary; Clarity is business language; OnboardingRisk is requirements; Likelihood increases with skip and fast value; Trust is an unexaggerated time promise. Suggestion: cut steps, skip, template, sample data, action verb CTA, reduce modals.

## JTBD Connections

The busy professional's small job is "produce this output today". The big job shouldn't stay generic like "be more efficient all the time". Timeline is written in small job language. If paywall comes before the small job, busy persona churns.

## Busy Professional Timeline Example

1) Looks for time and work outcome in the hero. 2) Gets stuck at signup form. 3) Can't skip the tour. 4) Leaves before seeing first value. Score rationale follows this sequence.

## Team and Individual Paths

Busy wants to start individually; if the product forces team invite, it applies a collaboration tax. Default should be individual, team optional. B2B admin comes later.

## Microcopy

Good: "Start in 2 steps with a template", "Skip tour", "Connect this later". Bad: "Let's get to know your company first", "Grow your team", "Explore our platform". Suggestion: keep microcopy in work and time language.

## Notification and Interruption Tax

For the busy professional, every modal and every email is a tax. If default notifications are high, weekly retention suffers. FirstClick suggestion is not "more engagement" but "less interruption, preference center".

## Calendar Reality

Two-minute tasks between meetings are critical. If there is no approval, rejection, or quick edit on mobile, the busy persona gets stuck in the timeline. This overlaps with the mobile document, but the rationale is time.

## Honesty of Time Promise

If the "5-minute setup" promise contradicts with form and tour, both busy and skeptical personas drop. Suggestion either validates the promise (by cutting steps) or reduces it in the text. Do not invent new unproven time claims.
