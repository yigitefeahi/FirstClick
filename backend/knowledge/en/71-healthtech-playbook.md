# Healthtech Sector Playbook

This file gains importance when the **healthtech** sector package is selected in FirstClick analysis. Each section can be retrieved as an independent RAG piece; each section contains the sector name, diagnostic questions, and score impact. Do not write fabricated statistics, conversion rates, or definitive regulatory statements. Heuristics are explicitly marked as heuristics.

## Scope and Business Model Context (healthtech)

Healthtech covers appointments, telehealth, clinical workflow, wearable data, symptom journaling, or wellness. Revenue may be B2C subscription, clinical license, or transaction-based. The critical distinction is between clinical claims and wellness/auxiliary tools. For diagnosis/treatment promises, FirstClick does not make medical accuracy decisions; it flags exaggeration and lack of evidence. Due to sensitive data, privacy requirements are stricter than general SaaS.

If there is no emergency guidance, a note on missing security communication may be added. If wellness speaks like clinical, it harms clarity+trust. For AI assistants, boundaries and human guidance are expected.

## First Impression Questions (healthtech)

Is it clinical or wellness? Is there diagnostic/treatment language? Who is the target? Is the evidence concrete? Who owns the data? Is there an emergency warning? Skeptical personas react to miracle claims; non-technical personas to jargon; busy clinicians to slow flows. Good: "Follow-up notes — does not diagnose." Weak: "AI doctor, diagnosis without lab."

## Onboarding and Activation (healthtech)

Activation: appointment, measurement sync, symptom entry, patient card. Long anamnesis requires progressive disclosure. If permissions come before features, distrust increases. Role selection should be early in clinical B2B. Analyst does not suggest diagnoses.

## Price, Trust, and Compliance Warnings (healthtech)

Package, cancellation, and 'expert' definitions must be clear. Seek transparency in drug/device implications. If there is no KVKK+health data disclosure/deletion, trust↓. Foreign compliance logos are weak without explanation. AI 'not medical advice' text must not contradict UI.

## Persona Reactions (healthtech)

Skeptical: miracle before/after. Price-sensitive: hidden session. Non-technical: jargon. Busy clinician: form intensity. First-timer: data anxiety. No medical advice.

## Drop-off Timeline Patterns (healthtech)

Unclear landing → long form → unjustified permission → unclear package → technical tele friction. Stages: Consent, Form, Appointment. No rates.

## Evidence to Seek in Corpus (healthtech)

Category, claim statements, emergency warning, privacy, evidence sources, appointment, roles, AI boundaries, price. Contradiction: says does not diagnose vs diagnostic language.

## FirstClick Score Impacts (healthtech)

Clarity: category clarity. Trust: claim humility+privacy. OnboardingRisk: form/permission. Adoption: first appointment/measurement.

## Action Checklist (healthtech)

1) State boundaries. 2) Trim miracle claims. 3) Emergency warning. 4) Stage forms. 5) Clarify data purpose. 6) AI boundaries. 7) Role onboarding. 8) Price conditions. 9) Name evidence. 10) Cite.

## Deep Diagnosis: Claim Discipline (healthtech)

Definitive outcome claims are high distrust candidates. Good language focuses on recording/preparation. Before/after is not a substitute for clinical evidence. FirstClick does not decide on efficacy.

## Deep Diagnosis: Clinical B2B (healthtech)

Look for multiple roles, appointments, notes, access control. Team promise contradicts UI if only one role. If there is no patient portal promise, it's a missing signal.

## Analyst Writing Template (healthtech)

Example: "Hero '[quote]' treatment promise; skeptical distrust; trust↓; shift to auxiliary tool language."

## Additional Scenario Matrix (healthtech)

Scenario A — wellness habit: clinical language leaks; subscription transparency.  
Scenario B — tele-appointment: package conditions, clinician qualification, emergency warning.  
Scenario C — clinical B2B: roles, audit, patient portal.  
Scenario D — AI symptom assistant: boundary text, human guidance, exaggerated diagnosis.  
No medical efficacy decision in any scenario; claim discipline and privacy are present.

## False Positives / Negatives (healthtech)

False positive: penalizing every medical term as jargon — if the target is clinicians, clinical language may be appropriate; if it mixes with patient language, confusion arises.  
False negative: assuming a privacy policy link is sufficient — if purpose is missing in the permission dialog, there is a gap. Praising miracle before/after as 'social proof'.
