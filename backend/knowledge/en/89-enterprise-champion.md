# Enterprise Champion Persona Depth

This document explains how to model the champion who brings the product into the organization in B2B within FirstClick. The champion is an enthusiastic user but cannot purchase alone; they must convince the economic buyer and security. Sections are independent.

## Purpose and Scope

The champion feels daily pain, wants to try, advocates internally, looks for a "why us" package, and wants ready answers to IT and finance questions. Persuasion: fast proof of value, shareable ROI summary, security FAQ, role-based demo or trial, a one-pager that can be forwarded to internal stakeholders. Deterrents: demo-only sales calls, lack of internal stakeholder material, SSO uncertainty, legal uncertainty, slide-focused sales without end-user value.

Out of scope: assuming the champion is the final buyer, writing a seller script. In scope: role within the committee, PLG+enterprise hybrid, scoring, and toughQuestions.

## Behavior Model

The champion first wants to solve their own problem (self-serve or quick trial). Then they bring a short justification to their manager. Next, they fill out the security form or forward it to IT. FirstClick corpus looks for where this chain breaks.

In champion language, "my team will use this" is present. If the landing page only speaks in C-level slogans, the champion does not see themselves. Conversely, if it only uses individual consumer language, they cannot make the internal sale.

## Concrete Examples

Example A — Strong trial but no security page. Champion gets stuck internally. Suggestion: trust center or SSO/KVKK FAQ; avoid making up features.

Example B — Only "book a demo." Champion wants to try over the weekend. Add a PLG path or interactive demo.

Example C — No ROI calculator; there is a case but not in a shareable format. Champion wants a short summary to forward via email.

Example D — Product works for end users; no admin invite. Champion cannot showcase to their team.

Example E — Sales says "we'll handle it"; price is not transparent. Champion struggles when transitioning to the economic buyer.

## Diagnostic Questions

Can the champion try self-serve? Is there shareable proof (ROI, security, case)? Is there a page for SSO/security questions? Is role-based access explained? Is demo the only path? Is there language tailored to internal stakeholders? Is end-user value visible? Does the price range make the champion's internal conversation easier? Is there a legal/KVKK link? Was there committee friction in previous analysis?

## Response Patterns

Pattern 1 — There is proof, but no veto material. Add trust center/FAQ; generate toughQuestions.

Pattern 2 — Demo-only. Champion cannot try. Add self-serve or interactive trial.

Pattern 3 — Individual language. Internal sale is hard. Make team and admin value visible.

Pattern 4 — Price is completely hidden. Makes internal justification harder. Show range or package logic.

Pattern 5 — No admin. No invite/role. Add team path.

Pattern 6 — Exaggerated ROI. Loses skeptical economic buyer. Use honest metrics.

## Anti-patterns

Assuming the champion is the sole decision-maker. Demo-only sales calls. Postponing security questions. Fake reference customers. Discount-focused script for the champion. Neglecting end-user value. Merging committee roles into a single persona. Claiming SSO exists when it does not.

## FirstClick Prompt and Score Effects

Prompt: champion job, internal sales blockers, stakeholder material in corpus, security signal, score, suggestion. PersonaReaction: how do I explain this to my team, what will security ask, is there a trial?

Score: Adoption is the champion's proof; Trust is veto readiness; B2B role separation; Likelihood increases with self-serve+material; Price eases internal conversation. Suggestion: trial, shareable summary, trust FAQ, role/invite, demo alternative.

## Champion Success Definition

Success for the champion is not just their own aha moment, but internal "go-ahead" approval. FirstClick suggestion sometimes points not to the product, but to a trust page similar to sales enablement. This is not outside the product; it is part of the purchase path.

## Bridge Between PLG and Enterprise

Good hybrid: champion tries without a card or with low risk, sees value, downloads the security package, forwards to the economic buyer. Bad hybrid: no trial, early sales pressure, unanswered security questions. Timeline writes this bridge step by step.

## Internal Email Template Test

Can three sentences that the champion would forward be extracted from the corpus? "What does it do, how long does it take, what is the risk?" If not, the message is not champion-friendly. Suggestion is to place these three sentences on the landing page.

## Role Conflict

Champion wants speed; security slows down. If the product only talks about speed, veto risk grows. If it only talks about security, champion excitement dies. A two-layered message is needed.

## Internal Sales Package Content

The package the champion forwards contains a problem statement, short demo or screenshot, cost range, security question link, and a success metric. How many of these does the corpus provide? Missing pieces are suggestion priorities; softening all of them as "add sales deck" is wrong.

## Transition from Trial to Committee

If the trial remains individual, the champion sees value but there is no team proof. Admin invite and role display make this transition easier. Should be read together with the end-user/admin document.

## Sales Pressure Anti-signal

If every CTA is "book a demo," the champion cannot try over the weekend. If there is no PLG bridge, B2B likelihood drops. Suggestion: interactive trial or at least post-signup value.
