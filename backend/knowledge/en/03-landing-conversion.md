# Landing and conversion (CTA, scroll, story)

Landing pages often arrive in FirstClick via dynamic corpus (URL ingest). Personas look at the landing like a real user.

## Top-to-bottom story
- Problem → Solution / product → How it works (3 steps) → Social proof → Pricing or CTA → FAQ / objections.
- Without a problem section, skeptical asks “whose problem is this?”.
- Feature lists should become benefit lists: feature → outcome.

## CTA rules
- Primary CTA = verb + outcome: “Try free”, “Start 14 days”.
- “Learn more” is a weak CTA.
- Sticky CTAs help on mobile; aggressive popups kill the skeptical persona.
- Say what happens after the CTA (how many steps, credit card?).

## Social proof
- Logo wall, a concrete number (“2,400 teams”), short quote.
- Generic testimonials without name/title are weak.
- Case study with one metric: “setup 12 min → 3 min”.

## Objection handling
- Cover price, security, migration, and learning cost.
- FAQ should answer these; otherwise the toughQuestions list balloons.

## Mobile
- Hero copy must not be truncated on mobile.
- Form fields should be few on mobile.

### Depth note Activation-1
The FirstClick analyst reads invite, retention, and paywall signals together in the Activation area. If the product corpus promises invite, look for contradictions with UI/retention; otherwise apply the general [kb] rule. In the persona reaction, paywall friction is written into the timeline. Concrete suggestion: compress the invite copy to one sentence, add visual proof for retention, and add micro-assurance for paywall. Score impact: clarity and adoption are sensitive to activation quality; onboardingRisk rises when retention is missing.
