# First 10 seconds: first impression and clarity

Core rule for FirstClick persona analysis: if the user does not understand the product in the first 10 seconds, the score drops. This knowledge base is used when linking persona reactions to the product corpus.

## Value proposition
- One sentence: who it is for, which problem, what outcome.
- Bad: “AI powered next-gen platform for modern teams.”
- Good: “Sprint boards for 5–20 person teams in 2 minutes — without Jira weight.”
- Test: read the sentence to a stranger; if they cannot answer “what does it do?” within 5 seconds, it is not clear.
- If there is no benefit next to the product name, the landing page loses.
- Jargon (“orchestration”, “synergy”, “seamless”) breaks trust on first impression, especially for non-technical and first-timer personas.

## Hero hierarchy
- Headline = outcome promise
- Subcopy = how / who it is for
- CTA = one primary action
- Support = social proof or a 15s demo
- Secondary CTA (“Watch demo”) should carry less visual weight than the primary.
- 3+ CTAs in the hero = decision paralysis; adoption score drops.
- On SaaS that hides price in the hero, the price-sensitive persona gets suspicious immediately.

## Visual–copy alignment
- Screenshots should be real product UI; stock illustration breaks trust (skeptical).
- Writing “one-click setup” while showing a 7-step wizard creates a citable contradiction: the persona flags it as drop-off.
- An empty dashboard screenshot kills the aha moment.

## Persona-specific first 10 seconds
- busy-professional: “what do I gain?” — wants time and ROI signals.
- non-technical: jargon-free language, visual guidance.
- skeptical: wants proof / logos / numbers; slogans are not enough.
- price-sensitive: free trial / clear limits.
- student: price and social proof.
- first-timer: category explanation + a “for beginners” path.

## Analysis tips (FirstClick)
- When writing persona firstImpression, quote the hero sentence from the product corpus.
- If promise and UI conflict, produce confusion + high friction on the Landing timeline step.

## Extended expertise notes

### Depth note Activation-1
The FirstClick analyst reads invite, retention, and paywall signals together in the Activation area. If the product corpus promises invite, look for contradictions with UI/retention; otherwise apply the general [kb] rule. In the persona reaction, paywall friction is written into the timeline. Concrete suggestion: compress the invite copy to one sentence, add visual proof for retention, and add micro-assurance for paywall. Score impact: clarity and adoption are sensitive to activation quality; onboardingRisk rises when retention is missing.
