# HR and Recruiting Sector Playbook

This file gains weight in FirstClick analysis when the sector package **HR / recruiting** is selected. Each section can be retrieved as an independent RAG piece. Do not invent statistics or write definitive regulatory provisions.

## Scope and Business Model Context (HR / recruiting)

HR/recruiting can include job posting, ATS, candidate CRM, assessment, payroll-adjacent tools, or internal communication/HR self-service. Revenue may be based on job slot, seat, usage, or success fee. In FirstClick, tension between two personas is common: recruiter/HR vs candidate. If the pitch is about 'candidate experience', the candidate flow is central; if it's about 'HR efficiency', the recruiter panel is central. Be careful with filter language that risks discrimination—no legal ruling, but transparency and candidate communication are evaluated.

This playbook customizes general SaaS rules when selected in the FirstClick sector package. Analysts do not invent numerical sector benchmarks; they rely on product corpus evidence. Heuristics are clearly marked and score justifications are written with citations.

## First Impression Questions (HR / recruiting)

For whom—company or candidate? What business outcome (speed, quality, fit)? Career site integration? Price per slot/seat? KVKK (Turkish data protection law) for candidate data? Busy recruiter seeks speed; skeptical candidate fears ghosting; price-sensitive SME looks for slot cost.

If category and outcome sentence cannot be established in the first 10 seconds, clarityScore is suppressed. CTA hierarchy should have a single primary action. If there is social proof, it should be specific; a generic logo wall may be weak.

## Onboarding and Activation (HR / recruiting)

Recruiter activation: first job post, first pipeline, first assessment. Candidate: completing application, strengthening profile. Mandatory career site integration can cause early drop-off. An empty pipeline without suggestions is weak. Long application forms on the candidate side create friction.

Name the activation event in product language. If the empty state does not show the next step, onboardingRisk increases. Make mandatory integration/form chains separate events on the timeline. If there is skip or progressive disclosure, note it as a positive signal.

## Price, Trust, and Compliance Warnings (HR / recruiting)

Job slot, promotion, and CV database access should be transparent. 'Unlimited posting' should not contradict with a footnote limit. Candidate data retention and deletion should be explained in the context of KVKK. Fees/cost per candidate for assessment tests should be clear.

Claims of percentages and guarantees without evidence violate citation discipline. If the KVKK/privacy link and the UI permission/purpose text contradict, trust↓. For enterprise promises, look for SSO/audit/role signals; if absent, note as missing signal, do not give a legal ruling.

## Persona Reactions (HR / recruiting)

Busy recruiter: slow job posting wizard. Skeptical hiring manager: unsubstantiated 'AI perfect match'. Price-sensitive SME: hidden slot. Candidate first-timer: unclear process status. Non-technical HR: ATS jargon.

Corpus citation is mandatory for persona reactions. Do not force personas that do not fit the product. ToughQuestions should be concrete and answerable; no insults or speculation.

## Drop-off Timeline Patterns (HR / recruiting)

Unclear landing side → long company form → inability to publish job → candidate faces 12-page application → silent status. Stages: Employer setup, Job post, Candidate apply, Pipeline.

Add rates and percentages. Write stage name + type of friction (confusion/friction/distrust). Week-1 silence can be an early qualitative signal.

## Evidence to Search in Corpus (HR / recruiting)

Role CTA, price, job posting form, pipeline, candidate status email, KVKK, AI matching claim, career page, assessment. Contradiction: job post in 2 minutes vs 30 fields; AI match vs inexplicable score.

If no evidence, say 'not found'. For contradiction findings, place two citations side by side. Do not carry evidence from competitor sites.

## FirstClick Score Impacts (HR / recruiting)

Clarity: side+outcome. Adoption: first job post/application. OnboardingRisk: heavy setup. Trust: candidate communication, KVKK, AI explainability.

Justify the score with a one-sentence slogan. Separate clarityScore, adoptionScore, onboardingRisk, and trust impacts. Promise-UI contradiction can affect multiple scores together.

## Action Checklist (HR / recruiting)

1) Clarify side. 2) Shorten job posting form. 3) Candidate status transparency. 4) Show slot price. 5) KVKK deletion path. 6) Explain or soften AI claim. 7) Empty pipeline CTA. 8) Assessment transparency. 9) Postpone integration. 10) Cite.

Recommendations should be actionable and prioritizable. Each recommendation should be linked to a finding. Instead of general advice like 'do better', write a concrete UI/text change.

## Candidate Experience Timeline (HR / recruiting)

On the candidate side, drop-off usually comes from long applications, mandatory account creation, unclear status, and perception of ghosting. FirstClick does not invent a "ghosting rate"; it checks for status emails/templates and the existence of a candidate portal. If the "apply in 2 minutes" promise contradicts with an 8-page form, both adoption and clarity are affected. If the duration of the assessment test is not shown in advance, busy candidate friction is noted.

## Recruiter Pipeline Quality (HR / recruiting)

Recruiter activation is the first job post + first candidate moved. If the empty pipeline is just decorative kanban, it's a weak empty state. If the AI matching score is inexplicable, skeptical hiring manager generates toughQuestions; FirstClick does not judge fairness/discrimination, but looks for explainability and human override signals. If career site integration is mandatory, an alternative manual job posting path is a heuristic.

## KVKK and Candidate Data (HR / recruiting)

Look for language in the corpus about candidate CV retention period, deletion request, and third-party sharing. If the business model is lead sales, transparency is essential; if hidden, distrust. Analysts do not make legal rulings. For the economic buyer, slot price and CV database access limits should be clear.

## Dual Journey of Employer and Candidate (HR / recruiting)

In HR/recruiting products, FirstClick should not settle for a single persona: recruiter/HR and candidate experience should be read separately. If the pitch says "perfect candidate experience", look for application form, status transparency, and communication templates. If the pitch says "HR efficiency", look for job posting wizard, pipeline, and assessment panel. If both are promised but one is missing, a clarity+trust tension arises.

Candidate drop-off heuristics: mandatory account, 8+ page application, unclear "application received" followed by silence, unexpectedly long test. Recruiter drop-off: long company KYC, mandatory career site integration, empty pipeline empty state. FirstClick does not invent ghosting rates; it checks for status and templates. If AI "perfect match" score is inexplicable and there is no override, skeptical hiring manager generates toughQuestions—no judgment on fairness/discrimination is made.

## Price, KVKK, and Lead Model (HR / recruiting)

Job slot, promotion, CV database, and success fee should be transparent. "Unlimited posting" should not contradict with a footnote limit. Candidate data retention period, deletion, and third-party sharing should be explained in the context of KVKK; if lead sales are hidden, distrust. Assessment test cost/duration for the candidate should be visible in advance. If total cost of ownership (slot + seat + add-on) is unclear for the economic buyer, objections increase.

## HR Playbook Score Summary

ClarityScore is sensitive to side+outcome clarity, AdoptionScore to first job post/application completion, OnboardingRisk to heavy setup and long forms, Trust to candidate communication+KVKK+AI explainability. Timeline: Employer setup, Job post, Candidate apply, Assessment, Pipeline. Do not write findings without citation.

## Assessment, Interview, and Employer Branding (HR / recruiting)

If assessment and interview scheduling features exist in the corpus, check if duration, purpose, and result communication are clear to the candidate. Vague metrics like "cultural fit score" generate toughQuestions for skepticals; FirstClick does not measure culture, it requires explainability. Employer branding page promise may be weak without career site integration. If internal HR self-service (leave, payroll document) is in the same product as recruiting, IA should not be mixed; otherwise, clarity drops.

If there are promises of reference check, offer management, and onboarding handoff, look for post-pipeline empty states. If absent, note as a missed opportunity—do not claim as mandatory feature. If mobile candidate application is just a shrunken desktop form, non-technical friction arises; qualitatively evaluate thumb and field labels.

## ATS Integration and Internal Candidate (HR / recruiting)

In combined ATS/CRM products, the existing candidate pool appears empty if not imported into the "new system"; if no import, onboardingRisk. If there is no internal job posting/employee referral promise, note as missing signal. Promise of multi-language job posting contradicts with single-language form. EEO/diversity data collection rationale should be transparent—analyst does not claim legal compliance. Offer approval chain (compensation band) may be critical for economic buyer; if absent, note sales-led B2B tension.

Sample toughQuestions in FirstClick HR analysis: "When does the candidate see their status?", "How does it explain and override the AI score?", "How long is CV data retained?", "What happens to the job post when the slot ends?". Answers should come from the corpus.
